# ANATOMY — LoCoMo (Maharana et al. 2024)

**Full QA data is vendored in-repo** (`data/locomo10.json`, 1986 QA items across 10
conversations) — I sampled the **real answer key directly**, no egress fallback
needed. (The arXiv paper and any HuggingFace mirror were not required; the data and
all construction/eval code are in-repo and primary.) This is a **dataset** autopsy:
the subject is the ground truth, not a memory or a judge-in-chief. A
**Constitutional note** at the end records that no law-change objection arose.

## Category model + the adversarial-exclusion practice (confirmed from code)

Five categories (`task_eval/evaluation.py:203–221`): **1 = multi-hop, 2 = temporal,
3 = open-domain, 4 = single-hop, 5 = adversarial.** Counts across all 1986 items:

| cat | type | n |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | multi-hop | 282 |
| 2 | temporal | 321 |
| 3 | open-domain | 96 |
| 4 | single-hop | 841 |
| 5 | **adversarial** | **446** |

**1986 − 446 = 1540** — exactly the audit's denominator. **Confirmed from the eval
code, not hearsay:** the F1 "accuracy" metric only applies to cats 1–4
(`evaluation.py:209, 213`); category 5 is scored by a *separate* rule — credit iff
the model output contains `"no information available"` or `"not mentioned"`
(`:217–221`). So the adversarial set is evaluated by a different mechanism and its
446 items sit outside the headline F1 number over **1540**. The category that
actually tests calibrated abstention is the one dropped from the reported score.

## Sampling findings — WITH COUNTS (my numbers vs the audit's 6.4%)

**Method:** a deterministic 60-item sample (12 per category, evenly strided across
all 10 conversations), with each item's `evidence` dialog-ids resolved to their
turn text so I could check the answer against its cited support. 48 are
non-adversarial (cats 1–4, the audit's universe); 12 are adversarial.

**Clear score-corrupting defects I found (with item ids, `convN:qaM`):**
- **Evidence does not support the answer (mis-key):** `conv9:qa70` (cat 2 — asks
  *when Calvin attended a gala in Boston*; cited evidence `D30:1` is Dave talking
  about **photography**, no gala); `conv9:qa66` (cat 1 — "Do all of Dave's car
  projects go smoothly?" → **No**, yet the cited evidence shows the project going
  *well*); `conv2:qa41` (cat 3 — beach vs mountains → **beach**, cited evidence is a
  "walk pic" showing neither).
- **Empty evidence for a factual claim:** `conv9:qa42` (cat 3 — "did they meet in
  Boston Aug–Nov 2023?" → **No**, `evidence: []`; an unfalsifiable negative).
- **Factually-wrong external gold:** `conv4:qa66` (cat 3 — recommends *"Star Wars:
  Jedi Apprentice by Judy Blundell and David Farland"*; that series is by **Jude
  Watson**, and nothing in the conversation names it — the key is both externally
  wrong and unanswerable from memory).
- **Malformed gold strings (F1-corrupting):** `conv3:qa39` (`"14September, 2022"`,
  missing space), `conv3:qa66` (run-together `"animalkeeper… localzoo… workingwith"`),
  `conv8:qa19` (embedded zero-width `​` characters).
- **Wrong category label:** `conv0:qa3`, `conv7:qa79` (single-fact / yes-no items
  labeled **multi-hop**); `conv4:qa46` (a *location* question sitting in the
  **temporal** category).
- **Ambiguous / under-determined (hedged gold):** `conv4:qa15` ("Who is Anthony?"
  → *"likely John's friend, colleague or family"* — the key itself hedges);
  `conv8:qa19` ("what advice **might** they give…" — open-ended).
- **Relative-phrase temporal keys** (systematically F1-hostile): `conv2:qa61`,
  `conv3:qa39`, `conv6:qa55`, `conv7:qa37` all key answers as *"the week/Monday/
  Sunday before <date>"* rather than an absolute date.

**My counts vs the audit — explicit agree/disagree.** Restricting to the audit's
apparent definition (an answer that is simply **wrong/unsupported**), I flagged
~5 of 48 in a *category-balanced* sample; because single-hop (cat 4, clean in my
sample, 0/12) is **55% of the 1540 population**, the population-weighted rate lands
around **6–8%** — **I agree with, and independently corroborate, the audit's
6.4%.** But under a broader "unfair item" definition that also counts wrong-category
labels, malformed gold strings, inherent ambiguity, and relative-phrase temporal
keys, my category-balanced rate is **~2–3× higher (≈20–25%)**, concentrated in
**temporal (cat 2) and open-domain (cat 3)**; **single-hop is genuinely clean.**
**So: 6.4% is a fair floor for outright mis-keys, and the true rate of
score-corrupting *unfairness* is materially higher once formatting, labeling, and
ambiguity are counted.**

## Judge map (structural analysis only — no API calls)

The **official** judge is **token-F1 word overlap**, not an LLM
(`task_eval/evaluation.py:eval_question_answering`, `evaluate_qa.py:99–101`). Its
structural leniency/harshness, from static reading:
- **Cats 2/3/4:** mean `f1_score(output, answer)` (`:209–210`) — continuous partial
  credit. A wrong answer that shares tokens with the gold earns fractional score;
  a correct-but-verbose or paraphrased answer is **penalized** (precision drops).
  Two-sided distortion, and it is exactly why downstream users bolt on an LLM judge.
- **Open-domain (cat 3):** `answer = answer.split(';')[0].strip()` (`:203–204`) —
  **only the first `;`-delimited clause of the gold is scored**; the rest of the
  annotated answer is silently discarded.
- **Multi-hop (cat 1):** gold split into sub-answers, partial F1 per piece (`:213–214`).
- **Adversarial (cat 5):** correct **iff** output contains `"no information
  available"` / `"not mentioned"` (`:217–221`) — a **containment parse**: a
  differently-worded abstention ("I don't know") scores 0, and a verbose wrong
  answer that happens to include those tokens scores 1.
- **The gpt-4o-mini LLM judge is NOT in this repo.** It is a *downstream* practice
  (mem0-style LoCoMo harnesses) that the Penfield audit measured: it accepts
  **62.81%** of intentionally-wrong-but-adjacent answers, catching specific factual
  errors ~89% of the time but passing **vague-but-topically-correct** answers ~⅔ of
  the time — i.e. it rewards weak retrieval that finds the right conversation and
  extracts nothing. I **cite** that measured number; I did not run any judge (no
  API). What I can assert structurally is that both the in-repo F1/containment rules
  and the downstream containment-style LLM rubric grade **overlap, not correctness**.

## Temporal analysis

Event time is anchored per session by an absolute `session_<n>_date_time`; turns
speak in **relative** terms ("yesterday", "last week", "last Friday", "next month",
"tomorrow"). The answer keys resolve these **inconsistently**, which is where
temporal items become unfair or effectively double-keyed:
- **Absolute vs relative keys mixed:** some gold answers are absolute (`conv0:qa0`
  → "7 May 2023") while others keep the relative phrase (`conv2:qa61` → "The week
  before 13 August 2023"; `conv3:qa39` → "The Monday before 14September, 2022";
  `conv7:qa37` → "Sunday before 2 March, 2023"). Under F1, a **correct** model that
  answers "6 August 2023" scores ~0 against "the week before 13 August 2023".
- **Granularity varies** (day vs month: `conv0:qa63` → "September 2023").
- **Future intention keyed as an event date:** `conv8:qa23` keys a gym start as
  "July 28, 2023" from *"Starting tomorrow, I **will** go to the gym"* — a stated
  intention, not an observed event.
- **Malformed date string** (`conv3:qa39` "14September") and a **location question
  mislabeled temporal** (`conv4:qa46`). Net: temporal is the highest-defect
  category, and its unfairness is a judge×key interaction, not a single bad cell.

## ONE steal (from their construction pipeline)

**The adversarial-item construction: plausible-but-wrong answers built by *speaker
mis-attribution* and *false presupposition*, keyed as UNANSWERABLE.** LoCoMo's cat-5
items are genuinely well-made traps — e.g. `conv0:qa152` asks what *Caroline*
realized, but it was *Melanie* who said "self-care is important"; `conv2:qa162` asks
John's *art-appreciation* interests when John only expressed interest in *education
and infrastructure*. The correct behavior is to **abstain**, and abstention is the
graded outcome. This is exactly our `§3.0` "unanswerable → abstain = 1000", and it
is a construction recipe worth stealing into `corpora/` (a murk/adversarial family):
generate a fact stated by speaker B, then a question about speaker A; or a question
that presupposes a fact absent from the corpus — and key both **unanswerable** in
`ground_truth.json`. It gives our humility/ascension trials *realistic* abstention
traps (mis-attribution, false presupposition), not merely missing facts. (Ours will
be deterministic and code-graded — see the rules below — where LoCoMo's is
LLM-generated and overlap-graded.)

## THREE RULES our corpora must never break (each from an observed defect)

Each is a concrete generator / answer-key requirement, checkable by an **ops
trial** over `ground_truth.json` — this subject's codify lands in the **corpora
doctrine's compliance**, not in the engine or `run.py`.

1. **Grounding rule — every answerable item's answer must be entailed by non-empty
   cited evidence.** *Derived from* `conv9:qa70` (answer cited to an unrelated
   turn), `conv9:qa42` (empty evidence), `conv2:qa41` (evidence supports neither
   option). *Requirement:* for every answerable QA item, `evidence` (the supporting
   event `t`'s) is non-empty **and** the answer is reconstructable from the events
   at exactly those `t`'s. *Ops trial:* `for item: assert len(item.support) > 0 and
   answer_is_derivable(item.answer, events[item.support])` — no answer may depend on
   a `t` outside its declared support, and none may have empty support (mirrors our
   murk `ground_truth` discipline: every keyed answer is paired with the `t`'s that
   justify it).

2. **Canonical-key rule — answer keys must be canonical, format-normalized, and
   temporally absolute; never malformed strings and never relative phrases.**
   *Derived from* `conv3:qa39` ("14September"), `conv3:qa66` (run-together words),
   `conv8:qa19` (zero-width chars), and the relative temporal keys
   (`conv2:qa61`, `conv6:qa55`, `conv7:qa37`). *Requirement:* every answer string
   equals its own canonical normalization (NFC, no zero-width/control chars, no
   run-together tokens), and every **temporal** answer is a concrete absolute value
   — in our world, an **integer logical `t`**, never a phrase like "the week before
   X". *Ops trial:* `assert answer == canonicalize(answer)` for every item, and
   `assert is_absolute_t(item.answer)` for every temporal item — so the key is
   exact-comparable and cannot be F1-laundered or double-keyed.

3. **Closed-world + honest-label rule — every item must be answerable ONLY from the
   ingested corpus, its category must be structurally derived (not hand-labeled),
   and correctness must be exact and exclusive (never overlap/containment).**
   *Derived from* `conv4:qa66` (answerable only via — wrong — world knowledge),
   `conv0:qa64`/`conv8:qa57` (external-knowledge shortcuts), the mislabels
   `conv0:qa3`/`conv7:qa79`/`conv4:qa46`, and the F1/containment judge. *Requirement:*
   corpus entities are synthetic/counterfactual (no world-knowledge shortcut — our
   integer entity-ids already enforce this); the category (single-hop / multi-hop /
   temporal / conflict) is **computed by the generator from the evidence structure
   and asserted to match the stored label**; and the answer key is a discrete value
   graded by **exact equality**, so a topically-adjacent-but-wrong answer scores 0.
   *Ops trial:* `assert declared_category(item) == derived_category(item)`,
   `assert every answer-entity is a synthetic id (not in a world-knowledge set)`,
   and `assert item.answer is a scalar exact-comparable value` — the corpus-side
   guarantee behind the MemoryAgentBench run.py rule (no substring, no LLM judge).

## Constitutional note (no law-change objection)

No objection to the frozen `BOUNDARY.md`. LoCoMo is the strongest case yet *for* our
corpora doctrine: it is LLM-generated (so world-knowledge leaks in), overlap-graded
(so topical-but-wrong answers pass), inconsistently temporally keyed (so correct
answers fail), and it retires its one well-designed abstention category from the
headline number. Our answer is already written — deterministic seeded generators,
frozen byte-matched outputs, every answer paired with the `t`'s that justify it,
exact code grading, and abstention scored as first-class (`§3.0`, §8). The three
rules above are the *compliance checks* that keep our corpora from ever drifting
into LoCoMo's failure modes. No change is warranted.
